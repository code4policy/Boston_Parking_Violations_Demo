2026 Boston Parking Enforcement Plan

GOALS OF PARKING PLAN 2026:

The 2026 Boston Parking Enforcement Plan is designed to conduct an analysis of 2024 parking enforcement data and 2025 pedestrian and bicycle accident data to better inform the deployment of traffic officers throughout Boston. Our approach focuses on targeted enforcement, hazard mitigation, and clear recommendations for future parking policies.

Spatial Behavior Analysis of Repeat Parking Violators in Boston

Key Insight: Violations Driven by Place-Based Constraints, Not Signage Deficiencies

Across all offender groups, repeat parking violations in Boston are strongly place based and highly predictable. The repeated concentration of infractions in the same locations by both the same and different offenders suggests that violations are not primarily caused by unclear or degraded signage. Instead, offenders appear to be aware of parking restrictions and repeatedly choose to violate them due to convenience, necessity, or lack of viable alternatives. These findings indicate that signage improvements alone are unlikely to significantly reduce violations; more effective interventions may include targeted enforcement, demand responsive parking policies, or localized redesign of parking regulations in high stress zones.

Top 5 Parking Violators: Highly Concentrated, Habitual Activity

The five most frequent parking violators exhibit extremely localized behavior, with violations clustered tightly within a small number of adjacent blocks. Each individual repeatedly commits infractions in the same micro areas, indicating habitual use of specific parking locations rather than sporadic or exploratory behavior. This pattern suggests that violations are driven by routine daily activities such as commuting or regular visits where offenders knowingly accept the risk of citation.

Top 10 Parking Violators: Shared Hotspots and Environmental Pressure

Expanding the analysis to the top ten violators preserves the dominant spatial clusters observed among the top five. New offenders display similar location bound behavior, often overlapping in the same high pressure parking corridors. The convergence of multiple offenders in these areas points to shared environmental constraints, reinforcing the interpretation that violations are responses to localized parking demand rather than confusion about regulations.

Top 30 Parking Violators: Density Increases Without Behavioral Randomness

At the top thirty level, the map becomes visually denser due to the inclusion of more offenders, but the underlying behavioral structure remains unchanged. Violations continue to cluster within the same neighborhoods, and individual offenders do not display widespread citywide movement. This indicates that increased violation density stems from more participants engaging in the same constrained spaces, not from more opportunistic or unpredictable behavior.
